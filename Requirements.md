Submit a proposal for an individual reinforcement learning project that you will complete over approximately two months. You are encouraged to choose a topic that genuinely interests you. Your project may involve an original RL idea, an experimental study, a comparison of algorithms or environments, a reproduction with a meaningful extension, or an application of RL to a suitable problem.



In one coherent proposal, address the following:



Project title and motivation: What problem do you want to investigate, and why is it interesting or useful?
Research question: What specific question, hypothesis, or behavior will your project examine?
RL formulation: Clearly identify the agent, environment, observations or states, actions, rewards, and objective, as applicable.
Proposed approach: What algorithms, environments, datasets, libraries, frameworks, or existing implementations do you expect to use?
Experimental plan: What experiments will you conduct? What baselines or alternative approaches will you compare?
Evaluation: How will you determine whether the approach succeeds or fails? Identify suitable measurements, plots, demonstrations, or other evidence.
Scope and feasibility: Explain why the project can reasonably be completed within approximately two months.
Initial resources: List any papers, repositories, environments, datasets, or other resources that you have already identified.


You may use existing RL libraries, environments, pretrained models, and open-source implementations, but simply running existing code is not sufficient. Your project must contain a meaningful question, experiment, comparison, modification, or analysis of your own. Clearly identify and cite any external resources you plan to use.



Your proposal is an initial plan and may evolve as you learn more. You will not be penalized if later evidence requires you to revise your method or research question. Negative results are acceptable when they are supported by a careful experiment and thoughtful analysis.
